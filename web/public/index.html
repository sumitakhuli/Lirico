lirico.lrc — what it does

Small app. Big singalong energy.

  1. [00:18.95]

    It finds the song by itself.

    YouTube in Chrome or Edge, Spotify, any player in Windows' media controls. Messy video titles ("Full Video Song", "(Official Video)") get cleaned up.

  2. [00:24.10]

    Lives in your taskbar.

    It sits in the taskbar's empty space, even with auto-hide. Drag it near the taskbar or beside a window's minimise button and it snaps on like a magnet.

  3. [00:31.72]

    Right on beat.

    One line at a time, with a mint underline that follows the singing. Pause, skip, scrub: it keeps up within a tenth of a second.

  4. [00:38.06]

    Hindi in English letters.

    Hindi songs show in English letters by default. Prefer Devanagari? One switch. Make it yours: fonts, colours, size, hotkeys like Ctrl+Alt+Space.

[00:45.00] the clever bit

Music videos start slow. Lirico waits for the singer.

Lyrics are usually timed to the album track, so on a music video they'd run early. Lirico listens to what your PC plays, hears the first sung words and locks on. It all happens on your PC: nothing is recorded, saved or sent.

Windows says "Windows protected your PC"

That's SmartScreen being careful with a new app that isn't code-signed yet. Click More info → Run anyway. Lirico then lives in your system tray.

Does it collect anything?

Only anonymous counts (minutes used, songs found or not, app version) to see whether Lirico is helping. Never song names, lyrics or audio. Turn it off any time in Settings → Behavior. The website counts visits without cookies.

A song shows no lyrics

Lirico shows lyrics it can find with timing in the free LRCLIB database. Some songs aren't there yet; you'll see "No synced lyrics found" and it stays out of your way.

Is it open source?

Yes, the code is on GitHub. Issues and ideas welcome.
